Ankle, foot or knee injury

Sprain or fracture?

Most twisted ankles and knees are sprains, but telling them apart usually means a costly hospital visit. Emergency doctors use the Ottawa Rules to decide who actually needs an X-ray. This walks you through the same questions.

A guide, not a diagnosis. The rules were built for a clinician's exam; if you're unsure at any step, get checked. Nothing you enter is stored.

About the Ottawa Rules

Developed by Dr Ian Stiell and colleagues in Ottawa (ankle and foot rules 1992–93, knee rule 1995–96) to cut unnecessary X-rays. In pooled studies they catch about 98% of fractures: ankle and midfoot 97.6% (Bachmann et al., BMJ 2003), knee 98.5% (Bachmann et al., Ann Intern Med 2004). They are deliberately cautious: many people they send for an X-ray turn out to have a sprain.

Built by a physician (MBBS) as an educational prototype for Razorpay's Fix My Itch problem "Why is distinguishing sprains from fractures impossible without costly hospital visits?". Not medical advice; in an emergency call your local emergency number.
